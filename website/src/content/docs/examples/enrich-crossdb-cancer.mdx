A similarity built from one database can always be questioned: perhaps the structure it finds is an artifact of
how that one group curated its pathways, not real biology. The way to settle it is to hand the question to
independent curators. If a signal is real, teams that never coordinated should reconstruct it from their own gene
sets.

Cancer can be put to that test. MSigDB's canonical-pathways compendium (Liberzon et al., 2011) bundles 3,090
pathways from five separately curated sources: KEGG (Kanehisa and Goto, 2000), Reactome (Gillespie et al., 2022),
WikiPathways (Martens et al., 2021), BioCarta, and PID. Each names and draws its cancer pathways in its own way.
The question is whether they agree at the level of shared genes.

## The analysis, step by step

**Index and compare.** As in the [single-database example](/DBRetina/examples/enrich-cancer-hallmarks/), `index`
builds the feature index and `pairwise` scores every pair of the 3,090 pathways with the size-aware Ochiai
coefficient, writing the full similarity graph to `c2cp_DBRetina_pairwise/`.

```bash
DBRetina index -g c2cp.gmt -o c2cp
DBRetina pairwise -i c2cp -m ochiai -c 0
```

**Take the seed from one database.** The seed is KEGG's cancer pathways only, the 15 KEGG entries whose name
mentions a cancer:

```bash
grep -iE 'cancer|carcinoma|leukemia|melanoma|glioma' c2cp.gmt | grep '^KEGG_' | cut -f1 > kegg_cancer_seed.txt
```

The held-out truth, which the seed never sees, is the same kind of list drawn from the other databases: the 64
cancer pathways that Reactome and WikiPathways curated on their own.

**Recover across databases.** `enrich` ranks every pathway by how enriched its neighbourhood is for the KEGG seed.
`--exclude-module` drops the KEGG cancer pathways, so the ranking is everything else, the four other databases
included.

```bash
DBRetina enrich -d c2cp_DBRetina_pairwise --module kegg_cancer_seed.txt -m ochiai -c 20 --exclude-module
```

The strongest hits are cancer pathways from the other databases (names shortened):

```text

bladder_cancer                       WikiPathways  13   84         1.72e-19
breast_cancer_pathway                WikiPathways  14   134        6.13e-19

melanoma                             WikiPathways  14   179        4.17e-17
pi3k_akt_signaling_in_cancer         Reactome      19   126        ...

```

Ranked and read off in full, the recovered set spans both databases: WikiPathways contributes its bladder,
breast, head-and-neck, prostate, melanoma, lung, pancreatic, and colorectal cancer pathways, and Reactome
contributes its oncogenic-signaling pathways, PI3K-AKT, EGFR, and TGF-beta signaling in cancer, and the RB1-driven
loss of cell-cycle control.

## Is the agreement real?

Press **Recover an independent cancer pathway** on the graph above and step through them. Each blue or green
pathway was drawn by a different team, yet most of its gene-sharing neighbours are cancer pathways, so the KEGG
seed ranks it near the top. Measured over all of them: of the 64 cancer pathways Reactome and WikiPathways curated,
58 share enough genes to sit in the graph, and the KEGG seed separates them from the 2,847 other pathways with an
AUC of 0.728. Against a null of 2000 random seeds of the same size, the mean is 0.53, and not one random seed
reached the real value: 4.1 standard deviations above the null, permutation p below 0.0005. The AUC is lower than
the within-database test, and it should be, since independent curators split cancer into different, finer pieces,
so a perfect line-up is not on offer. What matters is that the agreement is far beyond chance, and that it points
at the pathways any oncologist would name.

## What this shows

The shared-gene structure of cancer is not an artifact of one database. It is reproduced by curators who never
coordinated, which is about the strongest evidence there is that DBRetina reads biology rather than bookkeeping.
The practical lesson follows: a module assembled in one resource can be used to search another, and `enrich`
carries the signal across.

## Reproduce it yourself

Everything runs from
[`usecases/enrich-crossdb-cancer/`](https://github.com/DBRetina/DBRetina/tree/main/usecases/enrich-crossdb-cancer)
in the repository (the `run.sh`, the analysis scripts, and the data all live there).

1. **Install DBRetina** (the [quickstart](/DBRetina/start/quickstart/)) and the Python packages the validation and
   figures use: `scipy`, `pyarrow`, `matplotlib`, `networkx`.
2. **Get the gene sets.** They are MSigDB's full canonical-pathways collection, `c2.cp.v2023.1.Hs.symbols.gmt`
   (KEGG + Reactome + WikiPathways + BioCarta + PID), from the
   [MSigDB downloads](https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb/), saved as `data/c2cp.gmt`.
3. **Run** `bash run.sh`. It indexes the compendium, builds the pairwise, writes the KEGG seed, runs `enrich`,
   measures the cross-database recovery against the random-seed null, and regenerates the figure and the network
   above. The validation prints:

```text
compendium: 3090 pathways from 5 databases | edges at ochiai>=20%
seed: 15 KEGG cancer pathways | held-out: 64 Reactome+WP cancer pathways

CROSS-DATABASE RECOVERY
  real AUC          = 0.728
  null AUC (2000x)  = 0.534 +/- 0.047
  4.1 SD above null   permutation p = 5.00e-04
```
